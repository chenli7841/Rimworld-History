# HR-011G: Historical Events and Local Stories

## Implemented data

The Eastern Zhou scenario currently defines six dated historical records and nine continuous historical-context records. Together they meet the requested set of 15. Context records have BCE start/end ranges and no invented occurrence date. The calendar tab lists contexts active in the current year with their source notes.

Dated records cover the Western Zhou crisis, the eastward move, Qin recognition, the death of King Hui of Xie, Jin's Quwo grant, and Zheng's Duke Zhuang chronology. The Xie entry is year-only and disputed. The Zheng accession is labelled as an Xinian-based scholarly reconstruction; it does not change polity state. Historical dates use astronomical years (`-770` = 771 BCE).

The 20 HRE local templates are data-driven and use the existing HR-006 `HistoricalEventScheduler`, `HistoricalEventDef`, and chronicle storage. Seventeen are enabled. They are considered on the current historical calendar at intervals, require a player map and colonist, honor per-template cooldown and appearance limits, and save the pending choice and runtime records with the existing game component. Choices can change actual map stocks, add a regular colonist, or update a registered community posture. Results enter the chronicle as fictional local experience with explicit provenance.

Three templates are intentionally disabled: marriage (HR-007 relationship validation is not yet connected), claimed relatives (no genealogy identity check), and family migration (the current map/legacy systems cannot atomically move people, community, region, genealogy, culture, and archive data). No migration is simulated by changing an ID.

## Sources and chronology notes

- Pengcheng Zhang, [“The Chronology of Western Zhou,” *Early China* 46 (2023)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/early-china/article/chronology-of-western-zhou/0D661C3365537C0D15AD896F72F9DF70), especially the discussion of Kings Ping and Hui of Xie, Qin, Jin, and Zheng. Zhang places King Hui of Xie at 779–759 BCE and proposes Zheng Lord Zhuang's yuan at 743 BCE; these are reconstructions, not universal agreement.
- Minzhen Chen and Yuri Pines, [“Where is King Ping? The History and Historiography of the Zhou Dynasty’s Eastward Relocation”](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/where-is-king-ping-the-history-and-historiography-of-the-zhou-dyn/), *Asia Major* 31 (2018), for competing accounts of the eastward relocation and Zhou kingship.
- Shiji, “Zhou Benji,” “Qin Benji,” and “Zheng Shijia”; *Zuo zhuan*, Lord Hui 24, for received accounts. Where sources conflict, the entries retain the evidence level and do not apply an irreversible world-state change.
- Edward L. Shaughnessy, [“Western Zhou Archaeology,” *The Cambridge History of Ancient China*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-ancient-china/western-zhou-archaeology/30D8536A273E98A06E835739655CFF70), and [“Bronze Vessels: Style, Assemblages, and Innovations of the Western Zhou Period,” *Oxford Handbook of Early China*](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34296/chapter-abstract/290748951), for bronze, ritual, inscription, settlement, and burial contexts.

## Validation and current limits

`HistoricalEventDatabaseReport` reports dated events, contexts, local templates, disabled entries, and storyline definitions when the scenario initializes. Runtime state includes Scribe-backed storyline stage/termination records, but **the five HRC storyline definitions are not complete in this revision**. The supplied HR-011G paste ends midway through HRC-004's third stage and does not include HRC-005 or the remaining requirements. The implementation does not invent those missing details; the database report warns that fewer than five storylines are defined.

The offered local-event choices are interactive and some have persistent game effects, but templates that depend on marriage, verified kinship, real migration, historical relic transfer, or culture/technology changes remain disabled or chronicle-only until their owning modules expose safe operations. No game launch or in-game save/load test was performed; validation here consists of the RimWorld-referenced C# build and XML/localization cross-reference checks.
